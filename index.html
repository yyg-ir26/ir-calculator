Maximum Allowable Contrast Dose (MACD)

Uses the weight and serum creatinine entered above (Cigarroa formula)
Enter weight and serum creatinine above to calculate.
MACD = (5 mL × weight in kg) ÷ serum creatinine (mg/dL), capped at 300 mL. Derived from cardiac catheterization data (Cigarroa 1989) and used as a practical guide to limit contrast-associated AKI risk. It does not replace clinical judgment, hydration strategy or eGFR-based risk assessment. Volume refers to iodinated contrast (not diluted).

Periprocedural Bleeding Risk Labs

SIR 2019 consensus lab thresholds for percutaneous image-guided procedures
SIR 2019 uses a stricter INR target when arterial access is needed. Ignored for high-risk procedures and chronic liver disease.
Enter INR and/or platelet count to compare against the SIR thresholds.
Examples: low bleeding risk (SIR Table 3)
  • Paracentesis, thoracentesis, nontunneled chest tube for pleural effusion
  • Superficial abscess drainage or biopsy (palpable lesion, lymph node, soft tissue, breast, thyroid, superficial bone such as extremities, bone marrow aspiration)
  • Catheter exchanges (gastrostomy, biliary, nephrostomy, abscess), including gastrostomy/GJ conversions
  • Transjugular liver biopsy
  • Nontunneled venous access and removal (including PICC); tunneled venous catheter and port placement/removal; tunneled drainage catheter placement
  • Dialysis access interventions; IVC filter placement and uncomplicated removal; diagnostic venography and select venous interventions of the pelvis and extremities
  • Diagnostic arteriography and arterial interventions (peripheral, sheath <6 Fr), embolotherapy
  • Lumbar puncture (listed as low risk with a caveat, see the note below)
  • Thoracic and lumbar facet joint injections and medial branch blocks; peripheral nerve blocks; joint and musculoskeletal injections; trigger point injections; sacroiliac joint injection and sacral lateral branch blocks
Examples: high bleeding risk (SIR Table 3)
  • Ablation of solid organs, bone, soft tissue and lung
  • Arterial interventions with a sheath >7 Fr, or aortic, pelvic, mesenteric or CNS arterial interventions
  • Biliary interventions (including cholecystostomy tube placement)
  • Catheter-directed thrombolysis (DVT, PE, portal vein); portal vein interventions; complex IVC filter removal
  • Solid organ biopsy; deep non-organ biopsy (e.g., spine, soft tissue in intra-abdominal, retroperitoneal or pelvic compartments); deep abscess drainage (lung parenchyma, abdominal, pelvic, retroperitoneal)
  • Gastrostomy/gastrojejunostomy placement; urinary tract interventions (nephrostomy placement, ureteral dilation, stone removal)
  • TIPS (chronic liver disease thresholds apply); intrathoracic and CNS venous interventions
  • Spine procedures with risk of spinal or epidural hematoma (kyphoplasty, vertebroplasty, epidural injections, cervical facet blocks)
Source: SIR consensus guidelines, Patel et al., JVIR 2019;30:1168–1184 (Part II), Tables 3 and 4. Low risk: correct INR to within 2.0–3.0 or less (for arterial access, <1.8 femoral and <2.2 radial); consider platelet transfusion if <20 ×10³/µL. High risk: correct INR to within 1.5–1.8 or less; consider platelet transfusion if <50 ×10³/µL. The INR is an upper limit adjusted to the procedure (for example, <1.8 may be acceptable for liver biopsy, <1.5 may be preferred before an aortic intervention, and <2.0 for catheter placement with a subcutaneous tunnel). Chronic liver disease (suggested thresholds, expert opinion): low risk, INR not applicable, platelets >20, fibrinogen >100 mg/dL; high risk, INR <2.5, platelets >30, fibrinogen >100 mg/dL. SIR suggests vitamin K 10 mg slow IV if INR >2.5, a dose of platelets if the spleen is large and the count is below threshold, and cryoprecipitate (1 dose under 80 kg, 2 doses over 80 kg) for low fibrinogen. Plasma and platelets should be used judiciously in cirrhosis, and AASLD advises against correcting the INR with plasma. Lumbar puncture appears in the SIR low-risk table, with a pointer to the discussion that cites a platelet threshold of 50 (AABB); many centers manage it as high risk. Screening labs are not routinely recommended before low-risk procedures unless the patient has bleeding risk factors or takes warfarin or heparin. Targets for catheter-directed lysis and complex IVC filter retrieval should be individualized. Warfarin: low risk target INR ≤3.0; high risk withhold 5 days to INR ≤1.8 (see the Holds tool). Classification of a specific procedure is the operator's judgment, and your institution may differ.

MELD 3.0 & MELD-Na

Pre-TIPS and liver disease severity assessment
Enter bilirubin, INR, creatinine and sodium (plus albumin for MELD 3.0).
MELD 3.0 (adopted by OPTN in July 2023) uses bilirubin, INR, creatinine, sodium, albumin and sex. Lower bounds: bilirubin, INR and creatinine are floored at 1.0; creatinine is capped at 3.0 (dialysis = 3.0); sodium is bounded 125–137; albumin 1.5–3.5. Classic MELD-Na caps creatinine at 4.0 (dialysis = 4.0) and applies the sodium adjustment when MELD >11. Scores are bounded 6–40. Not for acute liver failure. If your result differs from another calculator, check: dialysis status, the creatinine cap (3.0 for MELD 3.0 vs 4.0 for MELD-Na), the sodium and albumin limits, sex, age (under 18 uses a different equation), and whether the other tool reports MELD 3.0, MELD-Na or original MELD. The values used after limits are shown under the result.

Local Anesthetic Maximum Dose

Weight-based maximum dose with volume at your concentration
Enter weight to calculate the maximum dose.
Commonly cited maximum doses for infiltration (lower of the mg/kg dose or the absolute cap): lidocaine 4.5 mg/kg (300 mg) or 7 mg/kg with epinephrine (500 mg); bupivacaine 2.5 mg/kg (175 mg) or 3 mg/kg with epinephrine (225 mg); ropivacaine 3 mg/kg (200 mg); mepivacaine 4.5 mg/kg (300 mg) or 7 mg/kg with epinephrine (500 mg); chloroprocaine 11 mg/kg (800 mg) or 14 mg/kg with epinephrine (1000 mg). Published limits vary between sources and labels, so confirm against your institutional formulary. Consider ideal/lean body weight in obese patients and reduce doses in hepatic impairment, elderly or frail patients, and when injecting into highly vascular areas. Concentration: 1% = 10 mg/mL.
Suspected LAST (perioral numbness, tinnitus, seizure, arrhythmia): stop injecting, call for help, secure airway and give 100% oxygen; give 20% lipid emulsion (e.g., 1.5 mL/kg bolus then 0.25 mL/kg/min). Follow your institutional LAST protocol.

What to use when a patient reports a "caine" allergy
Class (cross-reactivity is within a class, not between classes)
ClassAgents

  • True IgE-mediated allergy to local anesthetics is rare. Many "reactions" are vasovagal syncope, anxiety, epinephrine effects (palpitations), or reactions to additives (methylparaben, metabisulfite).
  • No cross-reactivity between esters and amides. Esters cross-react with each other. Amides usually do not cross-react with each other, but it has been reported (e.g., lidocaine and mepivacaine).
  • Methylparaben (multi-dose vials) is metabolized to PABA, as are esters. Epinephrine-containing solutions may contain metabisulfite.

Confirmed amide allergy Ester agent (e.g., preservative-free chloroprocaine) or 1% diphenhydramine. Another amide only after negative skin testing and graded challenge.
Ester allergy (procaine, tetracaine, benzocaine, chloroprocaine) Preservative-free amide (lidocaine, bupivacaine, ropivacaine). Avoid other esters.
Methylparaben / preservative or sulfite reaction Preservative-free, single-use vial; epinephrine-free (sulfite-free) solution.
Palpitations / tachycardia with epinephrine (not an allergy) Plain (epinephrine-free) solution.

1% diphenhydramine: can be made by diluting the 50 mg/mL vial 1:4 with normal saline (1 mL + 4 mL = 10 mg/mL). It is effective for short, minor infiltration, but has a shorter duration than lidocaine, can sting or irritate tissue, and is sedating. Avoid large volumes. Document the reaction type and agent, and consider allergist referral for definitive testing.

Contrast Premedication (ACR Manual)

For prior allergic-like reaction to the same class of contrast agent
RegimenDosing
Elective, prednisone-based (13 h) Prednisone 50 mg PO at 13 h, 7 h and 1 h before contrast, plus diphenhydramine 50 mg IV/IM/PO 1 h before.
Elective, methylprednisolone-based (12 h) Methylprednisolone 32 mg PO at 12 h and 2 h before contrast. Diphenhydramine 50 mg may be added (considered optional).
Accelerated IV (minimum 4–5 h) Methylprednisolone 40 mg IV or hydrocortisone 200 mg IV immediately and then every 4 h until contrast, plus diphenhydramine 50 mg IV 1 h before.

IV steroids have not been shown to be effective when given less than 4–6 h before contrast. Premedication reduces but does not eliminate the risk of reaction. Patients on chronic steroids may need adjustment. Emergent studies: a regimen with diphenhydramine alone is a last resort. Always have reaction-management medications and equipment available. Source: ACR Manual on Contrast Media; follow your institutional protocol.

Anticoagulant & Antiplatelet Holds

SIR 2019 consensus: how long to hold before a procedure
Select a medication.
Summary: high bleeding risk procedures
Low-risk procedures: SIR 2019 recommends continuing most anticoagulants and antiplatelets (warfarin: target INR ≤3.0). The exceptions are the glycoprotein IIb/IIIa inhibitors, which SIR lists as withheld for both risk levels.

DOAC timing: the SIR table gives DOAC holds in doses, not days. For twice-daily agents (apixaban, dabigatran) 2 doses is about 1 day; for once-daily agents (rivaroxaban, edoxaban) 1 dose is about 1 day. Renal tiers are CrCl ≥50, 30 to <50 and 15 to <30 mL/min as listed by SIR; where SIR gives no value, the tool says so.

Emergent procedures: SIR recommends reversal agents when a DOAC-treated patient needs a STAT or emergent high-risk procedure. Patients who cannot safely stop anticoagulation (e.g., recent coronary or cerebrovascular stents) need an individualized plan, and bridging decisions belong with cardiology/hematology. DOACs are not bridged.

Restart times assume the procedural bleeding risk has resolved (clot stabilization takes about 6–8 hours) and also depend on comorbidities such as chronic liver or kidney disease; multidisciplinary discussion may be warranted in high-risk patients. SIR notes the recommendations may not apply to emergency or highly urgent procedures where the risk of delay outweighs the bleeding risk, and they assume no other coagulation defect or interacting drug.

Cardiac stent on dual antiplatelet therapy (SIR Figure 2): for low-risk procedures, continue DAPT. For high-risk procedures, if the stent was placed less than 1 year ago, consult cardiology, vascular or internal medicine. If the stent is older than 1 year, continue aspirin; the second antiplatelet agent may be held 5 days before the procedure; consider consultation.

Neuraxial procedures: SIR cites multisociety guidance to stop factor Xa inhibitors 3–5 days and dabigatran 4–5 days before neuraxial procedures and to restart 24 hours afterward. This is a cautious strategy, and patients at high thrombotic risk may warrant bridging.

Source: SIR 2019 Table 6 (Patel et al., JVIR 2019;30:1168–1184, Part II) and Part I (Davidson et al., JVIR 2019;30:1155–1167). Every row was checked against the published tables; no newer SIR guideline was found as of October 2026. Confirm with your pharmacy and local protocol. Future Liver Remnant (FLR)

Standardized FLR (sFLR) for hepatectomy planning
Enter height, weight and FLR volume to calculate sFLR.
sFLR = FLR volume ÷ total estimated liver volume (TELV) × 100, where TELV = −794.41 + 1267.28 × BSA (m², Mosteller), from Vauthey et al. Commonly cited minimum sFLR: ≥20% in a normal liver, ≥30% after chemotherapy or with steatosis, ≥40% in cirrhosis. FLR volume alone does not assess function; consider liver function testing (e.g., ICG, HIDA), portal hypertension and the planned resection. Decisions on PVE belong to the surgical and IR team.

Hypertrophy After Portal Vein Embolization

Uses the height and weight entered above
Enter pre- and post-PVE FLR volumes and the interval.
Degree of hypertrophy (DH) = post-PVE sFLR minus pre-PVE sFLR (percentage points). Kinetic growth rate (KGR) = DH ÷ weeks since PVE. Commonly cited markers of adequate regeneration: DH ≥5 percentage points and KGR ≥2% per week (Shindoh 2013), together with reaching the sFLR target for the liver type above.

LSF = lung counts ÷ (lung + liver counts). Mean lung dose = 50 Gy·kg/GBq × activity (GBq) × LSF ÷ lung mass (kg), with 1 kg as the conventional assumed lung mass. Commonly applied limits: ≤30 Gy per treatment and ≤50 Gy cumulative. The 20% LSF ceiling is part of the resin microsphere instructions for use; glass microspheres use the dose-based limits. Hounsfield Unit Reference

Typical CT attenuation values (non-contrast unless stated)
Enter an HU value to see which tissues and materials commonly fall in that range.
Clinically useful thresholds
  • Hepatic steatosis (unenhanced): liver <40 HU suggests moderate-to-severe steatosis; liver at least 10 HU lower than spleen also supports it.
  • Adrenal adenoma: unenhanced ≤10 HU = lipid-rich adenoma. For lipid-poor lesions, washout at 15 min: absolute ≥60% or relative ≥40% suggests adenoma. Absolute = (PV − delayed) ÷ (PV − unenhanced) × 100; relative = (PV − delayed) ÷ PV × 100.
  • Renal mass enhancement (Bosniak v2019): increase <10 HU = non-enhancing; 10–19 HU = questionable; ≥20 HU = enhancing. Homogeneous hyperdense cyst: ≥70 HU unenhanced.
  • Macroscopic fat in a renal mass: ≥4 contiguous pixels <−10 HU suggests angiomyolipoma.
  • Emphysema: voxels <−950 HU (LAA-950).
  • Coronary calcium (Agatston): lesion ≥130 HU with area ≥1 mm².
  • Opportunistic osteoporosis screen (L1 trabecular, non-contrast): >160 HU generally normal; <100 HU suggests osteoporosis.
  • Urinary stones: uric acid usually <500 HU; calcium-containing stones usually >1000 HU.
  • Hemorrhage: acute clot is hyperdense (about 50–80 HU) relative to liver and spleen and decreases as it ages.
Values are approximate and overlap. HU varies with scanner, kVp, reconstruction, patient size, contrast phase and timing, and ROI placement. Contrast-enhanced values are protocol dependent. Use a representative ROI that avoids edges, vessels and artifact, and interpret in clinical context.
